A.) Explain your data preprocessing steps, i.e., why you introduce those steps.We implemented a strict chronological partition rather than randomized cross-validation, using 2021 through 2022 for model training and holding out all 365 days of 2023 for testing. In time-series forecasting, standard random shuffling allows future target information to leak backward into past training folds, which artificially inflates performance and fails to simulate true real-world operations. Inspection of the raw dataset showed exactly 1,095 consecutive rows with zero missing values, which allowed us to retain all entries without applying artificial numerical imputation. Finally, we standardized all continuous numerical features using a standard scaler fitted exclusively on the 2021–2022 training partition and applied to the 2023 test partition, which prevents large-scale variables from skewing gradient descent in the neural network and distance calculations in the kernel support vector regressor.

B.) Explain the features you choose and define, and argue their rationale.We engineered cyclical calendar features using sine and cosine transformations of both the day of the year and the day of the week to map recurring seasonal patterns onto a continuous circle. This mathematical encoding prevents the artificial boundary jumps that occur when treating December 31 as day 365 and January 1 as day 1. To model behavioral inertia, we constructed autoregressive lag features for one day prior, two days prior, and seven days prior, alongside a seven-day rolling average and standard deviation, ensuring all windowed features were shifted by at least one full day to prevent target leakage. We also defined compound weather interaction variables by multiplying precipitation by wind speed and outdoor temperature by humidity percentage. These interaction terms directly supply linear estimators with the compounding physiological discomfort cyclists experience in cold, wet, or windy conditions without forcing the models to extrapolate non-linear interactions from scratch.

C.) At least five different families of models must be included in this project, such as linear regression, MLP, and SVM.We selected and trained five distinct machine learning paradigms to compare linear, non-linear, ensemble, deep, and probabilistic learning representations. For regularized linear modeling, we implemented Ridge Regression with an L2 weight penalty. For tree-based ensembling, we utilized the Histogram-based Gradient Boosting Regressor, which constructs additive decision stumps over binned data. For kernel-based non-linear boundary estimation, we implemented Support Vector Regression with a Radial Basis Function kernel. For deep representation learning, we constructed a Multi-Layer Perceptron neural network featuring two hidden layers with 64 and 32 hidden units under rectified linear unit activations. For probabilistic learning, we implemented Bayesian Ridge Regression to treat model weights as probability distributions rather than fixed static coefficients.

D.) Choose appropriate objective functions (including regularizers) and optimizers, if your learning is done through optimization. If you use Bayesian methods, explain how you construct the learning.The Ridge regression model minimizes the empirical mean squared error augmented by an analytical L2 regularizer and is solved directly in closed form using Cholesky decomposition. The Histogram Gradient Booster minimizes squared error greedily using a second-order Taylor expansion with Newton-Raphson tree updates and shrinkage regularization. The Support Vector Regressor minimizes an epsilon-insensitive loss function with a margin parameter of 15.0 and an L2 penalty, optimized through Sequential Minimal Optimization. The Multi-Layer Perceptron minimizes mean squared error combined with an L2 weight decay regularizer of 0.01, optimized using the Adam optimizer with adaptive moment estimation and early stopping. The Bayesian Ridge model constructs its learning within an evidence framework, assuming Gaussian priors over the weight vectors and a Gaussian observation likelihood, iteratively maximizing the marginal log-likelihood via expectation maximization to estimate noise precision and weight hyperparameters directly from the training evidence.

E.) Train the models, and track the risk values of the objectives, or your posteriors in Bayesian learning.During training, we tracked convergence trajectories across all iterative estimators to verify stable risk minimization. The Multi-Layer Perceptron began at an initial loss of approximately 1,330,000 and dropped rapidly over the first twenty epochs, with early stopping halting optimization at epoch 94 once validation loss reached a steady floor. The Bayesian Ridge algorithm updated its hyperparameter posteriors over the evidence, converging in eight iterations to stable values of alpha near 0.0039 and lambda near 0.054. The Histogram Gradient Booster similarly achieved stable empirical loss reductions across its sequential tree boosting iterations without diverging or requiring early termination.  

 F.) Comprehensively and rigorously evaluate the performance of your models.All five model architectures were evaluated on the held-out 2023 test set across four standard metrics: Root Mean Squared Error, Mean Absolute Error, R-squared, and Mean Absolute Percentage Error. Bayesian Ridge Regression achieved the highest overall fit, recording a root mean squared error of 279.69, a mean absolute error of 221.04, an R-squared of 0.6793, and a mean absolute percentage error of 13.40%. Ridge Regression achieved nearly identical scores with an R-squared of 0.6790 and mean absolute error of 221.14. The Histogram Gradient Booster achieved an R-squared of 0.6558 with an error of 289.76, while the Multi-Layer Perceptron and Support Vector Regressor finished with lower R-squared values of 0.6386 and 0.6310, respectively. Evaluating a recursive seven-day rollout horizon demonstrated that rolling predictions forward stabilized around seasonal averages, recovering from an initial extreme day prediction to maintain error bounds between 20 and 236 rides across the week.   

G.) Interpret your models, such as which features are dominantly determining the prediction, if any.Permutation importance tests conducted on the held-out test partition demonstrate that outdoor temperature and rainfall are the primary variables determining bike rental counts, jointly explaining more than 80% of predictive variance. Ridge model coefficients indicate that precipitation induces an immediate downward penalty of over 250 rentals per day, whereas higher ambient temperatures generate strong positive demand gains. Marketing promotions provide a reliable positive secondary lift of approximately 86 rentals per day. Autoregressive history, primarily the seven-day rolling average and past weekly lags, acts as a stabilizing baseline that anchors demand across gradual seasonal transitions from month to month.   

H.) Argue the pros and cons of your models, and conclude which model will be deployed, if this is real.If deployed in production, the operator should deploy Bayesian Ridge Regression. The primary disadvantage of the Multi-Layer Perceptron and Support Vector Regressor is their tendency to overfit small tabular datasets, requiring greater compute and tuning while underperforming simpler linear alternatives. The tree ensemble handles non-linear boundaries well but yields discontinuous step-like predictions and lacks uncertainty estimates. Bayesian Ridge combines the lowest test error with near-instantaneous training on standard hardware. Most importantly for daily planning, it computes an analytical 95% predictive credible interval alongside every forecast, providing operations managers with an automatic risk flag when unfamiliar or volatile weather renders a prediction uncertain.   

I.) Are there any difficulties, issues, and challenges in your work? Explain them in detail.A key technical challenge was handling recursive multi-step forecasting across multi-day planning horizons, where true ground-truth rental numbers are absent and earlier model predictions must be recursively fed forward as inputs, creating the potential for compounding drift. A notable failure mode occurred during extreme compound weather anomalies, such as January 1, 2023, where near-freezing rain, high winds, and a holiday combined to force linear model predictions down to zero against an actual count of 220 rides. Furthermore, training the Multi-Layer Perceptron posed optimization stability challenges, as its high parameter capacity caused it to fit noise on the 723-sample training set, necessitating early stopping and weight decay penalties to achieve stable convergence.
